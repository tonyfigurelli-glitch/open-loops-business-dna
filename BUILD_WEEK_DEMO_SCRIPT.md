## Purpose

Provide a precise 2:30–2:45 public YouTube demo plan for the OpenAI Build Week **Work and productivity** submission. The recording should show the working product, explain how GPT-5.6 and Codex were used, and avoid unsupported claims or sensitive information.

## Recording Setup

Prepare the product before recording with fictional Northstar Studio data from the root README. Have these states ready:

- a new calibration at question 1;
- a completed deterministic-fallback session where the retry action is visible;
- a completed session with a saved successful AI-assisted retry;
- the AI-assisted result open at the ten narrative sections;
- Review details collapsed at first;
- browser chrome cropped or framed so local addresses, bookmarks, account names, and extensions are not visible.

Record the product window only. Use clean cuts between prepared states; do not wait for a live provider request during the final video.

## Narrated Script And Exact Screen Actions

Target runtime: **2:42**. Read naturally at approximately 128–135 words per minute.

### 0:00–0:16 — The Problem

**Screen action:** Start on the completed Business DNA result with the title and first two narrative cards visible. Slowly scroll a few pixels to establish that this is a working interface.

**Narration:**

> Small-business owners make important decisions while buried inside the work. Generic advice often mistakes a visible frustration for the real constraint—or presents a confident diagnosis without enough evidence. Open Loops Business DNA creates a more honest starting point.

### 0:16–0:34 — The Product

**Screen action:** Cut to the Open Loops home screen. Select the Business DNA calibration entry, then show the opening question and progress indicator.

**Narration:**

> In twelve focused questions, Business DNA learns what the business does, how the owner decides, what creates energy, what gets avoided, and what conditions support strong work. The result is provisional: an operating model to test, not a permanent label.

### 0:34–0:54 — Twelve Questions, Quickly

**Screen action:** Show question 1 with the fictional Northstar Studio answer. Use three quick cuts: one selection question, the 90-day outcome question, and the final business-principle question. End on question 12 showing “12 of 12.” Do not show typing in real time.

**Narration:**

> Each answer is saved immediately, and an incomplete session can resume later. The questions combine business context with tradeoffs: growth or stability, customer experience or internal efficiency, ideas or execution. They also capture the owner’s exact language without pulling in prior chats, account memory, or outside research.

### 0:54–1:23 — The GPT-5.6 Result

**Screen action:** Cut to the saved AI-assisted result. Show the AI-assisted success state, then scroll steadily through all ten titled narrative cards. Pause briefly on the strength-and-shadow, central-tension, respectful-challenge, and seven-day-experiment sections.

**Narration:**

> GPT-5.6 receives only the current session’s twelve-answer evidence package and returns a strict ten-section Business Owner Model. It moves from identity and strengths to a possible hidden cost, business consequence, leverage point, respectful challenge, and one reversible seven-day experiment. The writing is participant-facing, while evidence references stay in structured fields.

### 1:23–1:48 — Evidence And Uncertainty

**Screen action:** Scroll below the narrative. Open **Review details**, then open **Stored Interpretation**. Point to central hypothesis, confidence, unknowns, and possible disconfirming evidence. Briefly open **Original Answers**, then collapse both disclosures.

**Narration:**

> The system does not hide uncertainty. Major conclusions require independent evidence. Direct statements, inferences, hypotheses, unknowns, and competing explanations remain distinguishable. The original answers stay available for inspection, while confidence is calibrated instead of smuggled into the narrative as certainty.

### 1:48–2:15 — Fallback, Retry, Immutability, Refresh

**Screen action:** Use the session-history selector to open the prepared deterministic-fallback session. Show its fallback status and **Retry with GPT-5.6** action, but do not click it live. Cut to the prepared session with a successful retry. Show **Generation and Source**, collapse it, refresh the browser, then show that the AI-assisted success state and result remain selected.

**Narration:**

> If the provider times out, fails, or returns output that does not pass validation, deterministic fallback still completes the calibration. A retry reuses the exact stored twelve answers. It creates an append-only generation attempt rather than overwriting the completed session. Refreshing preserves the successful AI result, its provenance, failed attempts, and the immutable original model.

### 2:15–2:32 — How Codex Was Used

**Screen action:** Cut to a clean repository view showing `src`, `server`, the canonical Version 1.3 JSON, and test filenames. Do not show a terminal. Briefly show the README test commands and architecture section.

**Narration:**

> Codex accelerated the full engineering loop: repository analysis, the JSON-driven workflow, deterministic and GPT-5.6 generation, evidence validators, secure server boundaries, durable SQLite storage, retry recovery, production packaging, documentation, and regression tests. Frozen-file hashes and eighty-seven automated tests protect the approved calibration and its failure paths.

### 2:32–2:42 — Closing

**Screen action:** Return to the seven-day-experiment narrative card, then end on the Open Loops Business DNA title and public repository URL.

**Narration:**

> Business DNA does not promise a final diagnosis. It gives an owner a grounded model, visible uncertainty, and one small experiment that can produce better evidence. That is understanding turned into useful work.
